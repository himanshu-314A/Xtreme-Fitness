Dandasana
Dandasana, also called Staff Pose, is a foundational seated posture in yoga that helps establish alignment and strength throughout the body. In this pose, you sit on the floor with your legs extended straight out in front of you and your feet flexed, keeping the toes pointing upward.

Cobra Pose
Cobra Pose, known in Sanskrit as Bhujangasana, is a gentle backbend that strengthens the spine and opens the chest. To practice Cobra Pose, you begin by lying flat on your stomach with your legs extended behind you and the tops of your feet pressing into the floor.

Camel
Camel Pose, or Ustrasana in Sanskrit, is a deep backbend that stretches the entire front of the body while building strength and flexibility in the spine. To enter Camel Pose, you begin by kneeling on the floor with your knees hip-width apart and your thighs perpendicular to the ground.

Reverse Plank
Reverse Plank, known as Purvottanasana in Sanskrit, is a powerful yoga pose that strengthens the entire back body while opening the chest and shoulders. To practice Reverse Plank, you begin seated on the floor with your legs extended straight out in front of you and your hands placed slightly behind your hips, fingers pointing toward your feet.

Pigeon
Pigeon Pose, known as Kapotasana in Sanskrit, is a deep hip-opening posture that also stretches the thighs, groin, and lower back. To practice Pigeon Pose, you begin from a tabletop or downward-facing dog position and bring one knee forward, placing it behind your wrist, while the opposite leg extends straight back behind you with the top of the foot resting on the floor.

Plank Pose
Plank Pose, known as Phalakasana in Sanskrit, is a foundational yoga posture that builds strength, stability, and endurance throughout the entire body. To practice Plank Pose, you start from a hands-and-knees position and step your feet back one at a time until your body forms a straight line from the crown of your head to your heels.
